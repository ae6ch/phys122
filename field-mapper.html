About this model & how to use it

Fixed-voltage conductors obey Dirichlet conditions. The outer sheet edge has zero normal flux (Neumann). We solve ∇·(ε∇V) = 0 on a 221 × 171 grid using red-black successive over-relaxation and arithmetic face-averaged permittivity. Floating conductors are equipotentials constrained to zero total interface flux. E = −∇V, in V/cm. Orange paths use fourth-order Runge–Kutta integration. Finite grid resolution rounds sharp edges.

Insulators here model real dielectrics: the field passes through them. The bench voltmeter needs mobile surface charge, so its zero reading on an insulator is not a field measurement. A nonconducting hole in a resistive sheet also differs from a dielectric.

Click an existing object in any preset to edit it directly. Drag its body to move it, or a square handle to resize it. Change its properties in the selected-object panel; Delete/Backspace or Delete object removes it. New objects switch to Select automatically after placement. Editor: drag a plate, terminal, or insulator onto the sheet. Click vertices for a polyline, then press Enter or Finish. Select and drag objects to move them; drag the square handles to reshape. Escape cancels a drawing. Use the object list and numerical controls for keyboard editing; drawing tools also place an initial editable object with Enter on the focused sheet. Arrow keys move the probe. Ctrl/Cmd+Z undoes edits.

The measured contour levels come from the instructor’s lab videos. The dipole’s 1.6 and 8.3 V levels reflect probe clearance near the mounting hardware. Terminal feeds on plates are schematic; the solved plate geometry matches fields.py. Export saves the map with its legend and current arrangement title.
